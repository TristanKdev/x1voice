Phone orders are still a real slice of revenue for most restaurants, and if you run Square, the only question that matters about a voice agent is simple: does the order end up in Square correctly? A great-sounding voice that drops a modifier or sends the kitchen the wrong ticket is worse than a missed call.

Here's how X1 Voice works with Square, and what to check before you buy.

## The order has to land in Square, cleanly

X1 Voice connects to Square directly. It reads your live Square menu, prices every phone order the way your register already prices it, and drops the finished ticket back into Square as a normal order. Nobody re-keys anything. The value is in the round trip: [reading the live menu and firing a correctly-modified ticket](/blog/why-pos-integration-depth-matters-more-than-voice-quality), not just sounding human on the call.

The one thing to test with any vendor, Square or otherwise, is a deliberately awkward order — "no onions, sub fries, add a drink, apply the coupon" — and confirm it arrives in the POS right, not just that the voice repeated it back.

## Payments and reporting

Callers can pay by card on the phone for pickup and delivery. That payment reconciles alongside your other tender types, so your end-of-day numbers stay in one place. Staff never read a card number out loud or write one down.

## Setup

Connecting Square takes minutes, not a six-week project: link your account, import the menu, set your hours and any phone-only rules, run a test call, and go live. Most single-location restaurants are live the same day, and our team can handle white-glove setup if you'd rather.

If you run more than Square across locations, that's fine too — X1 Voice reaches [80+ POS systems](/integrations), directly or through Deliverect, so a mixed estate isn't a blocker.

You can read more about Square on their [official site](https://squareup.com).
