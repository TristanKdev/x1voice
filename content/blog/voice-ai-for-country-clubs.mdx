Four phone numbers ring in a typical clubhouse: the main line, the golf shop, the dining room, and the events office. Members dial whichever one they remember, which means every one of those phones fields questions meant for the others, and every one of them is answered by someone who was doing something else.

That is the real phone problem at a club. It is not a flood of missed orders like a pizza shop. It is a steady, low-volume trickle of calls that lands on people standing in front of members, at exactly the wrong moment, and gets handled with whatever attention is left over.

## What makes a club call different from a restaurant call

A restaurant's phone caller is anonymous and the conversation is about food. A club's caller has an identity that determines what they are allowed to do, and the conversation is about access as much as it is about dinner.

Is the grill open tonight. Can I bring three guests to Sunday brunch. What is the dress code in the main dining room. Does my minimum roll over. Can I book the private room for eight. Who do I talk to about my daughter's rehearsal dinner.

Answering those correctly requires knowing your club's rules, which are specific and written down somewhere, and often knowing who is asking, which is the part an agent cannot do on its own. Sorting those two categories is most of the configuration work.

## What the agent can genuinely handle

The rules half automates well, because your rules are stable and published. Configured properly, an agent gives every member the same answer, which is already an improvement over four phones staffed by people with different tenures.

- Dining room and grill hours by day, including the seasonal changes most clubs run and the days the kitchen is dark.
- Dress code by venue, stated in the club's own language rather than paraphrased, since this is a question people get touchy about.
- Guest policy as written: how many guests a member may bring, whether guest fees apply, and which venues admit guests at all.
- Standing information about the week, such as whether the Friday buffet is running and what the theme is, if someone keeps that current.
- Routing: sending golf questions to the shop, event questions to the events office, and billing questions to the office, without making the member call back on a different number.

That last one is quietly the most valuable. A member who dials the dining room to ask about a tee time currently gets transferred, or gets told to call back, and it registers as the club being disorganized. An agent that takes the question and routes it cleanly removes a small recurring irritation across your whole membership. The greeting matters here more than at a restaurant, since the voice is representing an institution people pay dues to belong to; [custom greetings and brand voice](/blog/custom-greetings-brand-voice-ai) covers what is adjustable.

## The member lookup limit, stated plainly

This is the assumption most club managers make and should not. At nearly every club, membership records, house accounts, minimums, and standing live in a club management platform that is separate from the POS running the dining room. A voice agent that writes orders into your POS has no window into that platform.

So the agent can ask for a member number and attach it to the order. It cannot confirm the number is real, check whether the account is in good standing, tell a member how much of their minimum is left, or flag that a family member is suspended. If a caller asks any of those, the honest configuration says a staff member will follow up and routes the call.

Whether that limitation matters depends on how your house accounts post today. If your POS already handles member charges and staff simply key a number, a phone order arrives in the same shape and completes normally. If someone has to look the member up manually before the ticket can close, the agent has saved the conversation but not the lookup. Ask your accounting office which of those two you run before you evaluate anything else. The parallel problem at hotels, where a room charge has to reach the folio, is the same structural issue and is described in [voice AI for hotel restaurants](/blog/voice-ai-for-hotel-restaurants-room-service).

## Events are a capture problem, not an automation problem

A club's event business, weddings, member parties, corporate outings, memorials, is often a large share of food and beverage revenue and it arrives by phone.

None of it should be automated. Deposits, contracts, member sponsorship requirements, date holds, and the negotiation over what is included all need your events director. What an agent should do is make sure that person never loses an inquiry.

- The date and whether it is firm, plus one alternate if the caller has one.
- Estimated headcount and event type, since a 40-person memorial lunch and a 180-person wedding go to different places in your calendar.
- Whether the caller is a member, is sponsored by a member, or is coming in cold, because at most clubs that determines whether the event is even possible.
- A callback number and the best window to reach them.

Event inquiries arrive on evenings and weekends, when the events office is closed and the person answering the clubhouse phone is a host with a line of members waiting to be seated. That is the call currently being handled worst at your club, and it is the most expensive one. ## Whether it is worth it at a club's volume

Do not run the missed-call arithmetic that works for a takeout restaurant. Your members will call back; they are not going to order from another club.

Run the interruption count instead. For one week, have whoever answers each clubhouse phone make a tally mark every time a call pulls them away from a member in front of them, and note what the call was about. Most clubs come out of that week with a page dominated by hours, dress code, and "is the grill open," which is precisely the set an agent answers without help.

Then decide against the actual cost. Plans [start at $250 a month](/pricing), setup is typically under a day, and the honest comparison is not against a salary you would otherwise pay. It is against the quality of member service you get from a host stand that stops mid-seating to answer a question about Sunday brunch.

If your tally comes back thin, skip it. Some smaller clubs really do run a quiet phone and a full-time office manager who handles it well. But if the page is full and half the marks landed during your Friday and Sunday rushes, you have found a service problem you were treating as a staffing problem, and the fix is cheaper than another body at the host stand. Set the escalation rules so anything about billing, a complaint, or a member's standing reaches a person immediately, following the pattern in [human handoff and failover](/blog/human-handoff-failover-voice-ai), and start with the dining room line only.
