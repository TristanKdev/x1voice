Toast is one of the most common POS systems in independent and multi-location restaurants, so "does it work with Toast" is usually the first question operators ask about a voice agent. It's the right question — but the honest version is more specific: does it read your live Toast menu, and does a finished order land back in Toast correctly? Everything else is secondary.

## Reading the menu, not rebuilding it

The foundation of a good Toast integration is that the agent pulls directly from your Toast catalog: item names, modifiers, and prices. That matters for two reasons. First, the agent only quotes things you actually sell today, priced the way your register prices them — no stale menu, no wrong totals. Second, you don't maintain a second menu. When you change an item in Toast, the phone agent reflects it. If a vendor requires you to keep a separate phone menu in sync by hand, you've signed up for drift and errors.

X1 Voice reads your live Toast menu, including modifiers and item-level customization, so the order it takes is priced against reality, not a copy that's a week out of date.

## The write is where value is won

Reading a menu is the easy half. The hard half — and the one that separates a real integration from a demo — is [firing a correctly-modified order back into the POS](/blog/why-pos-integration-depth-matters-more-than-voice-quality). "No onions, sub fries, add a drink, apply the coupon" has to arrive in Toast so the kitchen and the ticket match exactly. When that works, a phone order shows up in Toast as a normal ticket and routes like any other order; nobody transcribes anything.

This is the single thing to test before you commit with any vendor, Toast or otherwise: place a deliberately awkward modified order and confirm it lands in the POS correctly, not just that the voice sounded good saying it back.

## Payment and reconciliation

For pickup and delivery, callers can [pay on the call](/blog/collecting-payment-over-the-phone-safely), and that payment reconciles in your reporting alongside your other tender types. The operator doesn't read card numbers aloud or write them down, and the money lands where the rest of your day's revenue does.

## Beyond a single POS

Some groups run Toast at most locations and something else at a few. Because X1 Voice connects to Square and Clover directly and reaches [dozens more POS systems through Deliverect](/blog/deliverect-voice-agent-multi-location-restaurants), a mixed estate isn't a blocker — the phone experience is consistent even when the back-of-house systems aren't identical.

If you run Toast, the takeaway is simple: judge a voice agent on the round trip. A great voice that can't write a clean, modified ticket into Toast will cost you more than it saves. One that reads your live menu and lands correct tickets automatically is the version that actually earns its place on your phone.
