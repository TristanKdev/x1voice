Phone orders are still a real slice of revenue for most restaurants, and if you run Micros Simphony, the only question that matters about a voice agent is simple: does the order end up in Micros Simphony correctly? A great-sounding voice that drops a modifier or sends the kitchen the wrong ticket is worse than a missed call.

Here's how X1 Voice works with Micros Simphony, and what to check before you buy.

## The order has to land in Micros Simphony, cleanly

X1 Voice connects to Micros Simphony through our Deliverect integration. It reads your live menu, prices each phone order against it, and routes the finished ticket into Micros Simphony so it lands the same way an in-person or online order would. Nobody re-keys anything. The value is in the round trip: [reading the live menu and firing a correctly-modified ticket](/blog/why-pos-integration-depth-matters-more-than-voice-quality), not just sounding human on the call.

The one thing to test with any vendor, Micros Simphony or otherwise, is a deliberately awkward order — "no onions, sub fries, add a drink, apply the coupon" — and confirm it arrives in the POS right, not just that the voice repeated it back.

## Payments and reporting

Callers can pay by card on the phone for pickup and delivery. That payment reconciles alongside your other tender types, so your end-of-day numbers stay in one place. Staff never read a card number out loud or write one down.

## Setup

Connecting Micros Simphony takes minutes, not a six-week project: link your account, import the menu, set your hours and any phone-only rules, run a test call, and go live. Most single-location restaurants are live the same day, and our team can handle white-glove setup if you'd rather.

If you run more than Micros Simphony across locations, that's fine too — X1 Voice reaches [80+ POS systems](/integrations), directly or through Deliverect, so a mixed estate isn't a blocker.

You can read more about Micros Simphony on their [official site](https://www.oracle.com/food-beverage/).
