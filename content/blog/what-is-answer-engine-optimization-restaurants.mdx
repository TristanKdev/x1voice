Answer engine optimization, usually shortened to AEO, is the practice of structuring information so that systems which answer questions directly can find it, understand it, and repeat it correctly. Where traditional SEO was about earning a spot in a list of links, AEO is about being the source a system draws on when it just answers.

For a restaurant, this is less exotic than it sounds. It mostly comes down to making the factual basics about your business accurate, consistent, and readable by machines in every place they appear. That's unglamorous work, and it's most of the value.

## Why the shift is happening

For two decades, someone wanting to know whether you were open on Sunday typed a query, got ten links, and clicked one. The search engine's job was to route them to a page that probably had the answer.

Increasingly, systems answer directly. An AI assistant asked "is there a good Thai place near me open now" produces an answer rather than a list. A search results page shows a summary above the links. The user may never reach anyone's website.

That changes what "being findable" means. If a system is going to answer on your behalf, what matters is whether it has your information and whether that information is right. Ranking fifth on a page nobody scrolls to is a different problem than not existing in an answer.

## What AEO actually involves for a restaurant

Four layers, in descending order of how confident anyone can be that they matter.

### 1. Factual consistency (highest confidence, do this first)

Your name, address, phone number, and hours should be identical everywhere: your website, your Google Business Profile, delivery platforms, review sites, directory listings, social profiles.

When these disagree, any system trying to answer a question about you has conflicting inputs and has to pick. It may pick the old suite number, or the hours from before you extended them. This is the most boring item on the list and the one with the clearest payoff, because it also fixes the human version of the problem.

The most common culprits: an old phone number lingering on a directory, hours that were never updated after a change, and a location that moved but left a trail.

### 2. Structured data (high confidence, moderate effort)

Structured data is markup on your website that states facts in a format machines read unambiguously — this is the restaurant's name, these are the hours, this is the menu, this is the price range. The vocabulary most systems use is schema.org, and there are restaurant-specific types for exactly this purpose.

The value is that it removes interpretation. Instead of a system inferring your hours from a graphic on your homepage, you've stated them in a field labeled "hours."

### 3. Content that answers questions directly (moderate confidence)

If a page answers a real question in its first two sentences rather than building to it over six paragraphs, it's easier for any system to extract that answer. Same for FAQ sections that pose an actual question and answer it plainly.

This is also just better writing for humans, which is a reasonable sanity check on any AEO advice: if a tactic makes your site worse for people, be suspicious of it.

### 4. Reputation and mentions (lower confidence, harder to control)

Systems synthesizing an answer about your restaurant draw on more than your own site — reviews, local coverage, mentions elsewhere. You have limited direct control here, and the honest advice is the unsatisfying kind: be a restaurant people write positively about, and keep your review profiles claimed and current.

## What AEO is not

Some clarity on the boundaries, because this is a space with a lot of confident selling.

**It's not a trick to get mentioned.** There's no keyword to stuff. Systems that answer questions are generally drawing on what's verifiably true about you.

**It's not a substitute for being findable the ordinary way.** Your Google Business Profile still matters enormously for local intent. AEO is additive.

**It's not fully understood.** How these systems weight sources changes, isn't public, and differs across products. Anyone telling you exactly how to rank in an AI answer is claiming knowledge that isn't available. The defensible strategy is to make true information about you easy to find and consistent, which works regardless of how the weighting shifts.

## The restaurant-specific priorities

A few things matter more for restaurants than for businesses generally:

**Hours are the highest-stakes fact you publish.** They're the most-asked question and the one where being wrong sends someone to a closed building. Every place your hours appear needs to be updated together, which is why we recommend a [pre-holiday checklist](/blog/holiday-hours-overrides-voice-ai) that includes your listings and not just your phone system.

**Your menu should be readable text.** A menu that exists only as a PDF or an image is invisible to a lot of systems. If you want questions about what you serve answered correctly, the menu needs to be on the page as text.

**Delivery and pickup details are frequently asked and frequently stale.** Whether you deliver, to where, and your minimums — see [delivery zone rules](/blog/delivery-zone-rules-phone-orders) for why those are worth writing down clearly anyway.

**Your phone number has to be right and has to be answered.** This is the part that connects to everything else. Being surfaced in an answer produces a phone call, and a phone call that rings out is the [most expensive thing your phone does](/blog/the-real-cost-of-a-missed-restaurant-phone-call).

## A realistic starting checklist

Half a day of work, most of the available value:

1. Audit your name, address, phone, and hours across every listing you can find. Fix the disagreements.
2. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile, including hours and attributes.
3. Get your menu on your website as text, with prices.
4. Add restaurant structured data to your site.
5. Write plain answers to the questions you actually get: parking, delivery area, dietary options.
6. Make sure calls that result actually get answered.

Then stop and do something else, until there's better evidence about what more elaborate tactics achieve.

## The bottom line

Answer engine optimization for a restaurant is mostly the discipline of making true things about your business consistent and machine-readable. Fix your facts everywhere, add structured data, put your menu on the page as text, and answer common questions directly. That's durable work that pays off regardless of which systems people use to find you.
