## II. Scope and Methodology

### A. Defined terms

The following terms carry the meanings assigned here throughout this report.

- **Search Performance.** The realized outcome of the property in organic
  search, measured from Google Search Console and Google Analytics 4: rankings,
  impressions, clicks, click-through rate, organic sessions, engagement, and
  conversions.
- **Technical Site Crawl.** The condition of the property as retrieved by an
  automated crawler: availability, indexation controls, on-page structure,
  structured data, and response characteristics.
- **Answer and Generative Engine Optimization.** The readiness of the property
  to be found, trusted, extracted, and attributed by AI answer engines and
  generative search features. Abbreviated AEO and GEO respectively and assessed
  together.
- **Finding.** A graded statement of fact about the property, supported by
  measured evidence and paired with a remedy.
- **Severity.** The class assigned to a finding, governing its point deduction
  and its position in the remediation schedule.

### B. Weighting model

The master score is a weighted mean of the three prong scores. The full weights
are Search Performance 0.50, Technical Site Crawl 0.30, and Answer and
Generative Engine Optimization 0.20. The reasoning for each weight is stated in
full in `docs/METHODOLOGY.md`, Section 2. In summary, search performance carries
the most weight because rankings and the clicks and conversions they produce are
the realized result the discipline exists to create, measured against live query
data rather than inferred from markup. The technical crawl carries the next
weight because it is the controllable input that makes ranking possible. Answer
and generative engine readiness carries the least weight of the three because it
is at present a secondary acquisition channel, weighted to matter without
allowing a strong posture there to conceal weak search performance.

Because the search prong did not run in this issuance, its 0.50 weight was
redistributed to the two executed prongs in proportion to their base weights.
The technical crawl was reweighted from 0.30 to 0.60 and the answer-engine prong
from 0.20 to 0.40. This is the redistribution rule in Section 2.1 of the
methodology.

### C. Scoring of each prong

The technical crawl and answer-engine prongs are scored by exception. Each
begins at 100 and is reduced by the deduction fixed for each finding's severity:
critical 25, major 12, moderate 6, minor 2. The search prong, when it runs, is
scored quantitatively from six measured components. The full rubric is in
`docs/METHODOLOGY.md`, Section 3.

### Schedule of findings

**II.1 Sitemap last-modified dates default to build time.**

- Severity: Minor
- Evidence: The sitemap generator falls back to the build timestamp when a route
  omits an explicit last-modified date, and most entries in the static route
  table omit it. Every such URL therefore reports the same, uninformative change
  date on each build.
- Remedy: Emit each URL's true last-modified date, sourced from content
  frontmatter for articles and from a tracked edit date for static pages. An
  accurate freshness signal helps search engines schedule recrawls efficiently.

### Conforming items

- HTTPS is enforced and a Strict-Transport-Security header with a one-year
  max-age and preload is served on every response.
- robots.txt is served, permits crawling of public paths, disallows application
  and authentication paths, and declares the XML sitemap.
- The XML sitemap is generated from the same route table that renders the
  navigation, so it cannot list a page that does not exist or omit one that does.
- Every page emits a self-referencing canonical produced by a single metadata
  builder, which makes the canonical provably equal to the sitemap URL and the
  served URL. This removes the circular canonical and redirect condition that
  affected the prior site.
- Titles and meta descriptions are set per page through that builder, and the
  descriptions reviewed are specific and within the display range.
- Structured data is emitted across the site: Organization and WebSite once in
  the document head, and FAQPage, Article, Service, and BreadcrumbList on the
  pages to which each applies. The Organization node is declared once and
  referenced by identifier elsewhere, which removes the duplicate-identifier
  condition that affected the prior site.
- Open Graph and Twitter card metadata are present sitewide.

### Provenance

- Signals verified against the property's source of record: robots policy,
  sitemap generation, the shared metadata and canonical builder, the structured
  data builders and their use, and the response-header configuration.
- The following checks require a live crawl of the production host and are not
  reflected in this provisional score: discovery of broken internal links at
  scale, response-time distribution, orphan-page detection, and rendered-output
  parity across the full set of programmatic URLs. Run the crawl prong from a
  networked environment to complete them.

## V. Prong III: Answer and Generative Engine Optimization

**Prong score: 78 out of 100.**

The property is well constructed for answer engines at the access and structure
level and is held back by the credibility of some of its published content. It
permits every major answer-engine crawler, publishes a machine-readable page
index, emits the structured data those engines extract, and serves its
substantive content in the initial HTML rather than injecting it with script.
The deductions are for trust and entity signals, not for access or structure.
The controlling item is the presence of non-verified testimonials published as
customer reviews, which undermines the property's citation-worthiness and carries
independent regulatory exposure.

### Measured data

| Metric | Value | Benchmark |
| :-- | --: | :-- |
| Answer-engine crawlers permitted | 5 of 5 named | |
| Named crawlers allowed | GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended | |
| llms.txt published | Yes | Yes |
| FAQPage schema | Present | Present |
| Organization schema | Present | Present |
| Organization sameAs entity links | Absent | Present |
| Server-rendered content | Substantive, statically generated | |

### Schedule of findings

**III.1 Non-verified testimonials are published as customer reviews.**

- Severity: Major
- Evidence: The homepage renders a review wall from a single source array in
  which 25 of 30 entries are flagged in the source of record as illustrative
  samples rather than real customer feedback. The component that renders them
  does not filter the non-verified entries, and the source file itself carries an
  instruction that they must be replaced before launch. Answer engines weight
  content that is verifiable and attributable; unverifiable social proof
  presented as fact reduces the property's credibility as a citable source.
- Remedy: Replace the illustrative entries with real, attributable customer
  reviews, or remove them. This is also a consumer-protection requirement, not
  only a ranking consideration. Publishing fabricated testimonials as genuine is
  a deceptive practice under the applicable advertising rules.

**III.2 Organization schema omits sameAs entity links.**

- Severity: Moderate
- Evidence: The Organization structured-data node is emitted with name, URL, and
  logo, but its sameAs property is empty. Answer engines use sameAs to resolve a
  brand to a known entity across the web. With no sameAs edges, the property
  gives them nothing to reconcile it against.
- Remedy: Populate sameAs with the brand's authoritative external profiles, for
  example its verified business and social profiles and any industry directory
  listings. This is the single most direct improvement to entity grounding.

**III.3 The machine-readable page index inherits terse route descriptions.**

- Severity: Minor
- Evidence: The published llms.txt is generated from the route table and
  reproduces each route's description. Several of those descriptions are generic
  and very short, for example "What X1 Voice does." and "About X1 Voice." The
  file that answer engines read first therefore describes several pages in a way
  that carries little information.
- Remedy: Give each route in the route table a specific, factual one-line
  description. The rendered meta descriptions are already well formed; this
  applies only to the shorter strings that feed the sitemap and llms.txt.

**III.4 The core statistic carries no third-party citation.**

- Severity: Minor
- Evidence: The recurring "one in four calls goes unanswered" figure renders with
  an internal-estimate label and no external citation, because its source link is
  unset. The framing is honest, but answer engines discount uncited quantitative
  claims and are unlikely to attribute the figure to the property.
- Remedy: Cite a third-party source for the figure, or retain the honest
  internal-estimate framing and present it as a proprietary finding rather than a
  general fact.

### Conforming items

- Every major answer-engine crawler is explicitly permitted in robots.txt:
  GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, and Applebot-Extended.
- The property publishes an llms.txt that indexes its public pages, generated
  from the same route table as the sitemap so it cannot drift out of sync.
- FAQPage schema is emitted on the pages that carry question-and-answer content,
  which is the format answer engines most readily extract and attribute.
- Organization and WebSite schema establish the brand and site as entities.
- Substantive page content is present in the server-rendered HTML, so
  answer-engine crawlers that do not execute JavaScript still see it.

### Provenance

- Access and schema signals verified from the robots policy, the llms.txt
  generator, and the structured-data builders and their use across the source of
  record.
- The live answer-engine content-coverage probe, which measures whether the
  property's own text can answer representative buyer questions, was not run in
  this issuance. It requires an Anthropic API key and a live crawl. Run the AI
  prong from a networked environment with that key set to add the coverage and
  citation-readiness measurements to this section.

## VIII. Limitations and Reservations

This report is limited in the following respects, each stated so the reader can
weigh the conclusions accordingly.

1. The search-performance prong did not run. The most consequential measurements,
   rankings and the traffic and conversions they produce, are therefore absent
   from this issuance, and the master score is provisional. It should be treated
   as a grade of the property's foundation and answer-engine readiness only, until
   the search prong is connected and run.
2. The technical crawl and answer-engine findings were verified against the
   property's source of record rather than a live crawl of the production host.
   Every finding stated is supported by that source. The at-scale checks listed
   in the Prong II provenance require a live crawl and are not reflected in the
   provisional crawl score.
3. The live answer-engine content-coverage probe did not run. The answer-engine
   score reflects access, structure, entity, and trust signals, not a measured
   rate of question coverage.
4. Scores are produced by the fixed instrument stated in Section II and in the
   methodology. They are comparable across runs of this methodology and are not
   directly comparable to scores from any other tool or scale.
